-    <field name="scopecontent_teim">The Fred B. Renwick Papers contain materials that relate to the professional career of Fred Blackwell Renwick, a professor of finance who taught at New York University's Leonard N. Stern Business School from 1965 to 2001. Collection materials date from 1966 through 2015, with the bulk of the collection dating between 1982 and 2001. Materials include Renwick's 2015 New York Times obituary; resumes; documents about Renwick's honors, awards, and grants; conference materials; correspondence; as well as research files, some of which relate to Renwick's articles or his collaborative projects with other professors, including the chapters he wrote for A Bibliography of Investments, which he co-authored with Malek K. Lashgari, Associate Professor of Finance at the University of Hartford, in 1990. Renwick's research files are followed by a number of general course files consisting of handouts, reading packets, and exercises, which are arranged in the collection by topic. In addition to these general files, the collection also contains specific course files that are arranged in chronological order by term. Courses include Foundations of Finance / Introduction to Finance; Financial Markets and Investments; Equity Instruments and Markets: Analysis, Predictability, Appraisal; Security Analysis; Investment Principles and Analysis; and Portfolio Analysis. Also included in the Renwick Papers are similar course materials generated by other Stern faculty members (both paper and electronic), as well as a file of student coursework.</field>
-    <field name="bioghist_teim">Fred B. Renwick was a faculty member of New York University's Leonard N. Stern Business School from 1965 to 2001. Prior to teaching, Renwick earned a Bachelor's Degree from Morehouse College, S.M. from Harvard, MBA and Ph.D. in Finance from the Stern School of Business. While at NYU, his research interests included equity price and market predictability; behavioral finance; investments and finance eleemosynary institutions and corporations; security valuation, financial strategy, and portfolio and asset management; and models and analysis of speculative markets, both domestic and worldwide. In addition to teaching, he was also an accomplished author of published monographs, research articles, and a textbook. In 2007, a few years after retiring, Renwick even received an honorary Doctor of Humane Letters degree from Morehouse College in 2007. Renwick passed away at his home in Manhattan on  December 4, 2014, survived by his partner Lynne Mueller.</field>
