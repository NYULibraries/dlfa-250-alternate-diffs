     <field name="bioghist_teim">Penina Coopersmith attended New York University from 1965 to 1972, where she received a Bachelor of Arts degree. There she was active in the NYU branch of Students for a Democratic Society--managing its New York City office in the summer of 1966--as well as in other progressive political organizations.  Part of her political work included advising young men how to emigrate to Canada to avoid being conscripted to fight in the Vietnam War.  Her political interests also encompassed civil rights and labor union struggles. In 1966, for example, she attended a summer school in Illinois run by the Amalgamated Clothing and Textile Workers; the following winter, she traveled with a group of young people to observe a strike by textile workers at Erwin Mills in Dunn, North Carolina, and with Steve Max (an SDS founder) produced a report on the strike that was published by Youth Committee Against Poverty (YCAP).  She was also participated in the occupation of university buildings by students during the NYU student strike of May 1970.  In September, 1971, she moved to Toronto, Canada, and settled there, working as an urban planning and writer.</field>
