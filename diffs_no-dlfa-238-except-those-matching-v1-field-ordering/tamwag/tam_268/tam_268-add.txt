     <field name="bioghist_teim">Sol Yurick (1925-2013) was an American author based in New York City, who was best known for novels portraying poverty and urban life in the 1960s and 1970s. Born in Manhattan on January 18, 1925, Yurick grew up in the Bronx where he attended the Bronx High School of Science. After high school he enlisted in the United States Army where he trained as a surgical technician during World War II. Following his military service he enrolled in New York University and graduated with a BA in English in 1951. He later received an MA in English from Brooklyn College.
 Yurick worked for several years as an investigator for the New York City Department of Welfare. His first book, The Warriors, drew from his experience with the Department of Welfare and portrayed gang violence as a product of poverty rather than social dysfunction. Loosely based on the classical Greek story Anabasis, Yurick sought to provide a more accurate portrayal of gang violence that contrasted the glamorized images in pop culture such as West Side Story.  The Warriors was later turned into a motion picture in 1979, which garnered a strong cult following.</field>
